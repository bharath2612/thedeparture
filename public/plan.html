What we should build

The flight is the hook. The package is the business.

Air is 5–10% of a mature OTA's revenue; lodging is 70–80%. Our accreditation is not a licence to sell tickets — anyone can do that through a consolidator. It is a licence to obtain Inclusive Tour fares, which may only be sold bundled with a hotel, so the air component is never revealed. You cannot be undercut on a price nobody can see.

Differentiator 01

Search by the bags you're actually carrying.

Our customer flies Dubai to Kochi twice a year with forty kilos of gifts, food and things for family. They do not primarily care about the fare. They care about excess baggage — and no travel site prices a search around it. Move the dial and watch the winner change.

Differentiator 03

The booking happens in WhatsApp.

Metasearch is closed to us — CPC exceeds air margin before a single ticket clears. But the Gulf–India visiting-friends-and-relatives corridor is enormous, price-sensitive, flies the same route two to four times a year, and is served today by agents in Deira and Karama taking phone calls. Booking.com's funnel is web-shaped; it cannot follow a customer into a WhatsApp thread. Repeat purchase is the only thing that has ever made a consumer flight business work, and VFR is the only segment that reliably repeats.

Distribution

Three pipes. Only two of them now.

Ranked by share of corridor seats. The conventional order — sign a GDS, then bolt NDC on top — is wrong here. Our routes are nonstop and low-cost-carrier heavy, so the two things a GDS uniquely provides — interlining and multi-carrier fare construction — are things we barely use.

GDS

Deferred

Not yet, and on purpose

  • At launch volume the GDS charges us — subscription plus per-segment. Incentives reward volume we don't have.
  • Sign only when an airline agrees to file private fares to a PCC, or our volume clears the incentive threshold.
  • Until then, borrow a host agency's PCC for the few bookings that need one.

Not in v1

Deliberately deferred

  • A GDS contract — at launch volume we pay them, not the other way round
  • A second bedbank — the day you add one you owe a hotel-mapping layer
  • Virtual interlining — that is an insurance business, and it needs capital
  • Metasearch listings — the search meter costs more than the ticket earns
  • B2B agent portal
  • Anything outside the corridor
  • A chatbot

Not software

Worth more than the roadmap

  • Ask for a pilot, not for fares. No carrier files private fares to an agency with no volume history. Ask for a volume commitment and a six-month review.
  • Start with Air India Express and Air Arabia, not Emirates. The carrier whose alternative to you is worst has the most reason to say yes.
  • Lead with incremental demand — VFR passengers from a WhatsApp channel where the airline has no distribution. It is the only pitch that works, and it is true.
  • Contract fifty Dubai hotels directly. Takes you from 10–25% margin to 25–35% — on precisely the hotels this customer books.
  • Secure an acquirer early. Travel is MCC 4722; expect a rolling reserve. This gates launch more often than any API does.

What kills this

Three things, in order of speed.

Chargebacks
Cheap fares select for the worst cohort, and travel is the preferred cash-out for stolen cards. Your acquirer raises the reserve the moment the ratio ticks. That is an extinction event, not a bad quarter.
The float illusion
You collect instantly and remit to BSP on a cycle. You will sit on customer money owed to airlines and hotels, and it will look like a healthy bank balance for about a year. Never spend it.
No volume, no fares
The honest tax on going consumer-first: no carrier files good private fares to an agency with no volume history. You pay it down with repeat purchase in one dense corridor — which is the same plan, arrived at from the other direction.
